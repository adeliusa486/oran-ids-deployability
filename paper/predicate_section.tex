No O-RAN specification defines the bounds of Eq.~\eqref{eq:deploy}, so we chose them ourselves; an operator would set them from its own traffic and staffing. The generalization bound is $\delta = 0.10$ in balanced accuracy, applied to the upper end of the corrected 95\% interval. The alert term asks for a threshold that detects at least half of the attacks ($r_{\min} = 0.5$), at which at least one alert in ten is real ($\rho = 0.1$ at $\pi = 0.002$), and which raises at most $A_{\max} = 200$ false alerts per hour at $\lambda_{b} = 240{,}000$ benign flows per hour. All alert conditions are checked on \DB{}, where a detector would be deployed, with the threshold searched there too, so the alert term is the best a detector could reach. For latency we take $B = 10$\,ms, the strictest budget the specification allows. It was measured through a RIC only for window-level detectors with the same architectures (99th-percentile upper bound at most \RicLoopTenPnnHiMax{}\,ms), not for the flow-level detectors tested here (Section~\ref{sec:res:latency}), so Table~\ref{tab:predicate} reports $B_{\min}$ for the window-level loop and the verdict rests on the other two terms.

Table~\ref{tab:predicate} gives the verdict on all flows: no architecture passes (\PredPass{} of six). All fail the generalization term, with upper bounds of \PredGenMin{} to \PredGenMax{}, and all fail the alert term by a wide margin: at a recall of at least 0.5, the best operational precision on \DB{} is \PredPPVmax{}, and the fewest false alerts per hour are \PredFAmin{}. Each one of $\rho$ above \PredPPVmax{}, $A_{\max}$ below \PredFAmin{} per hour, or $\delta$ below \PredGenMin{} would on its own keep every architecture out. Nor does the verdict depend on the declared prevalence: the false-alert count does not involve $\pi$ and exceeds $A_{\max}$ at every threshold, and the precision condition alone would need $\pi \ge \PredPiStar$, \PredPiStarRatio{} times the declared value.

On the flows without the benign copies, with both terms computed on those flows, the verdict is also \PredCleanPass{} of six, but the generalization term fails for a different reason: the loss is significant for only \TrCleanSig{} architecture (Section~\ref{sec:res:transfer}), and the upper bounds of \PredCleanGenMin{} to \PredCleanGenMax{} exceed $\delta$ because the intervals are wide, not because a loss of that size is established. The alert term decides the verdict. At a recall of at least 0.5 the best operational precision is \PredCleanPPVmax{} and the fewest false alerts per hour \PredCleanFAmin; the precision condition alone would hold from $\pi = \PredCleanPiStar$, but the false-alert count rules out every architecture.

Fig.~\ref{fig:sensitivity} shows how far the bounds would have to move. On the flows without the copies, an architecture meets the alert term only if an operator accepts several thousand false alerts per hour at a precision of a few percent, and the generalization term only if $\delta$ exceeds \PredCleanGenMin{} (\PredGenMin{} on all flows). Latency would matter only once the first two terms were met; in our window-level loop it clears the smallest specified budget by about a factor of two.

\emph{Attainability of the test.} A test that nobody passes may just be too strict. The published 5G-NIDD pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:res:target}) shows where it can be passed, with two caveats: its generalization term is the drop from each classifier's own R0 score, a point estimate over two splits that is zero at R0 by construction, and its latency was not measured. On its own random split, \PubRzPassN{} of its five classifiers pass (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}), with operational precision up to \PubRzPPVmax{} and as few as \PubRzFAmin{} false alerts per hour, and on all flows none passes at a later rung. On the flows without the copies, \PubOneCleanPassModels{} still pass at R1 (random split, no record-position fields), and RF passes across base stations (R3) with the published features, which a deployment would not have. None passes with capture files held out (R2), nor at R3 without the record-position fields, where DT and RF meet the alert term but lose \PubThreeNoposCleanDropMin{} in balanced accuracy. The test can thus be passed within one corpus once the label conflict is removed, but not when whole capture files are held out. Under the evaluations closest to deployment that the available corpora allow, transfer to an independently collected corpus, the detectors in this paper fail.
