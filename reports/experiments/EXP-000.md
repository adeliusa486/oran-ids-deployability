## Research question

What is the measured state of this repository, what does the current literature
actually establish about O-RAN intrusion-detection deployability, and is gate A1
(the unidentified source corpus) resolvable?

---

## Baseline

Not applicable. An audit has no treatment arm. The comparator is the repository's own
claims about itself and the manuscript's own claims about the literature; the audit's
job is to find where those diverge from what a command or a retrieved source shows.

---

## Method

**Repository side.** Every statement of state was produced by a command, never by
reading the README. Four guards were executed and their output preserved verbatim in
`results/EXP-000/logs/`.

**Literature side.** Nine web searches and thirteen page retrievals, bounded to one
pass. Every matrix cell carries a `verification_status`. Where a source did not state
something, the cell reads `unknown`; `no` was used only where a source explicitly
indicated absence. This distinction is what keeps a keyword search from masquerading as
a systematic review.

### R2 — Gate A1 is provisionally resolvable

**NetsLab-5GORAN-IDD** (Abed Zadeh, Civciss, Wickrama Arachchilage, Liyanage, Sandeepa;
IEEE Data Descriptions 2025; DOI `10.1109/IEEEDATA.2025.3614167`; Zenodo 18923275;
CC-BY-4.0) supplies all three properties A1 requires:

- raw `.pcap` captured at the O-CU, plus Zeek `conn.log`/`http.log`/`files.log`
- 22 PHY/MAC radio metrics per record, exported from the O-DU over the E2 interface
- six labelled classes: Benign, DoS, DDoS, Probe, Brute Force, Web

from a physical OpenAirInterface O-RAN testbed (1 O-CU, 2 O-DU, 1 O-RU, Dell Precision
7920 O-Cloud edge server, 2 physical UEs) at University College Dublin.

This is the plan's option **A1-b**, the cheapest good outcome, and it preserves the
radio-KPI ablation that options A1-c and A1-d would have destroyed.

### R3 — The near-RT latency budget is a range, and the choice decides the verdict

| | P04 (ICCCN 2026) | P05 (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Budget assumed | 10 ms | 1000 ms |
| Latency measured | 1–5 µs inference, < 4 ms end-to-end | ~600–850 ms |
| Verdict | feasible | feasible |

O-RAN specifies the near-real-time control loop over **10 ms to 1 s**. Two studies pick
opposite ends and both conclude feasibility, five orders of magnitude apart.

Our manuscript asserts a single `B = 10 ms` and derives a binary predicate from it. As
written, the paper's central verdict is an authoring choice presented as a measurement.

### R4 — Half of our sharpest claim is already published, on our own candidate corpus

Fard, Komarov and Wunder (IEEE CSR 2026) run a seven-architecture, run-disjoint,
ten-seed cross-layer study on NetsLab-5GORAN-IDD and report that **radio features match
or exceed network-flow features in distribution**. That is the first half of claim C5.

### R5 — Cross-domain IDS failure is not novel in 2026

Hakim, Uddin and Anis (arXiv 2607.00553, 2026) report cross-network generalisation
failure for four lightweight IDS architectures over three IIoT corpora, on a shared
feature space, under naturally imbalanced distributions.

## Unexpected results

Three, in descending order of consequence.

1. **The blocker was resolvable in one literature pass.** The plan budgeted one week for
   option A1-b and rated the outcome uncertain. A single targeted search found a corpus
   published after the plan was written. The lesson is procedural: a "blocker" that has
   not been re-searched recently may be stale rather than hard.

2. **The candidate corpus arrives with prior work attached.** Adopting it means
   inheriting Fard et al. as the in-distribution reference and giving up half of C5.
   This is a real cost of the cheap solution and the plan's decision table did not
   anticipate it.

3. **The latency literature contradicts itself, and the contradiction is a contribution
   opportunity rather than a problem.** Sweeping `B` turns an arbitrary threshold into
   a measured crossing point, which is what an operator actually needs.

## Scientific interpretation

The project's stated blocker is no longer the binding constraint; **verification is**.
A corpus has been identified but not inspected, and the difference matters: record
counts, label columns, device and run identifiers, and CU–DU time alignment are all
unknown, and every one of them is a prerequisite for the split design (A4) and the
windowing policy (A12).

The literature pass also narrows the paper. Three of the four things the draft treats as
findings are, separately, known: radio features help in distribution (P03), cross-domain
transfer collapses (P14), the base-rate problem makes low-prevalence precision poor
(P13, 2000). What remains unclaimed by anyone is the **conjunction** — transfer, alert
burden and tail latency measured for the same artefacts under one protocol — plus two
genuinely open questions the literature raised rather than answered: whether radio
features that help in distribution *hurt* under transfer, and at what budget within the
10 ms–1 s range each architecture stops being deployable.

That is a smaller contribution than the draft implies and a more defensible one.
