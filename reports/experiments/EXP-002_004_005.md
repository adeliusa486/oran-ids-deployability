## 1. EXP-002 — How much does the split protocol inflate the score?

**Research question.** How much of a typical published NIDS number survives a
protocol that reflects deployment?

**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** H-leak: group-disjoint scores are materially lower
than random-split scores for every non-trivial model.
**Falsified if:** the 95% CI on the per-model difference includes 0 for all models.

**Primary metric.** Macro-F1, chosen before running, because this corpus is 76.5%
attack at the window level and plain accuracy is uninformative by construction.
CIs are percentile-bootstrapped over **split means**, not over individual runs —
the dominant uncertainty is which runs landed in which fold (plan A5).

### Result

| Model | Random split | Group-disjoint | Δ | 95% CI on Δ | CI excludes 0 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|:---:|
| hgb | 0.983 | 0.870 | +0.113 | [+0.027, +0.209] | **yes** |
| xgboost | 0.976 | 0.859 | +0.117 | [+0.029, +0.221] | **yes** |
| rf | 0.970 | 0.871 | +0.099 | [+0.009, +0.204] | **yes** |
| tree | 0.956 | 0.822 | +0.134 | [+0.048, +0.226] | **yes** |
| mlp | 0.953 | 0.872 | +0.081 | [−0.008, +0.186] | no |
| logreg | 0.835 | 0.802 | +0.034 | [−0.060, +0.127] | no |
| stratified | 0.499 | 0.508 | −0.009 | [−0.011, −0.006] | yes (negligible) |
| majority | 0.434 | 0.431 | +0.002 | [−0.009, +0.013] | no |

**H-leak is supported for 4 of 6 non-trivial models** and directionally consistent
for the other two.

### The finding that was not predicted

**Leakage scales with model capacity.** The ordering is monotone in flexibility: the
single tree and the three ensembles gain 0.10–0.13 macro-F1 from a random split,
the MLP gains 0.08, logistic regression gains 0.03, and the two trivial baselines
correctly gain nothing.

That ordering is the signature of *memorisation* rather than learning. A random
split lets a flexible model recognise the host or the capture run; a linear model
has too little capacity to exploit that, so it barely benefits.

The consequence is uncomfortable for the field and is worth stating plainly: a
published random-split number is inflated **most** for exactly the architectures
most likely to be published. It is not a uniform offset that can be mentally
subtracted.

### Sanity checks that passed

- The `stratified` baseline shows −0.009, i.e. no leakage to within 1%, which is
  what a random-guessing classifier must show. A non-zero result there would have
  indicated a bug in the split machinery rather than a finding.
- `LeakageError` is raised if a split claiming disjointness has any shared group.
  It did not fire.

## 2. EXP-004 — What does this detector cost an operator?

Criterion 2. `π` (attack base rate) and `λ_b` (benign flows per hour) are **declared
parameters**, not measurements — neither is observable from this corpus — so both are
swept and every result is also given per 10⁵ benign flows.

### At τ = 0.5, π = 0.002, λ_b = 240,000 benign flows/h

| Model | Macro-F1 | Precision **on the corpus** | Recall | FPR | Alerts/h | **PPV in deployment** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| mlp | 0.876 | 0.937 | 0.958 | 0.216 | 52,253 | **0.051** |
| hgb | 0.870 | 0.938 | 0.952 | 0.216 | 52,245 | **0.052** |
| xgboost | 0.860 | 0.936 | 0.944 | 0.221 | 53,446 | **0.058** |
| rf | 0.872 | 0.934 | 0.960 | 0.229 | 55,510 | **0.039** |
| logreg | 0.802 | 0.936 | 0.853 | 0.195 | 47,135 | **0.055** |
| majority | 0.431 | 0.759 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 240,481 | 0.002 |

**The two precision columns are the paper's Criterion 2 in one line.** Corpus
precision of ~0.94 becomes an operational PPV of 0.04–0.06 once a realistic base
rate is applied. Roughly 52,000 alerts per hour, about 1.25 million per day, of
which ~19 in 20 are false.

Nothing here is a new statistical insight. Axelsson established it in 2000. What is
new is the quantification for a specific O-RAN detector under a group-disjoint
protocol, reported alongside transfer and latency for the same artefacts.

### Second finding: the threshold knob does not work on the boosted models

Sweeping τ to recover precision:

| Model | τ for PPV ≥ 0.25 | Recall there | Alerts/h | τ for PPV ≥ 0.50 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| rf | 0.65 | 0.940 | 51,447 | 0.95 (→ 3,674 alerts/h) |
| xgboost | 0.60 | 0.934 | 52,437 | 0.99 |
| logreg | 0.85 | 0.520 | 21,528 | 0.99 (→ 819 alerts/h) |
| mlp | 0.75 | 0.923 | 45,419 | **unreachable** |
| hgb | 0.99 | 0.906 | 45,245 | **unreachable** |

HGB still emits 45,245 alerts/h at **τ = 0.99**, and PPV = 0.5 is unreachable at any
threshold for HGB and the MLP. Their scores are saturated near 1.0 under a
group-disjoint split, so the threshold — the operator's only runtime control — has
almost no effect. Random forest, by contrast, drops to 3,674 alerts/h at τ = 0.95.

This is a **calibration failure with a direct operational consequence**, and it is
not visible in any accuracy, F1 or AUC number. It belongs in the paper.

## 3. EXP-005 — Latency at the tail, against a swept budget

### Measurement class: EMULATED

Windows host, no CPU isolation, single-process Python timing. Every artefact is
tagged `measurement_class: emulated`. These numbers support the **relative ordering**
of architectures and the per-stage decomposition. They do **not** support a
conformance claim on real RIC hardware. Per decision D-005 the real measurement
belongs at Level 2 (real Near-RT RIC, synthetic E2 load) on a Linux host.

### The floor ran first

Platform p99 with a no-op model: **0.103 ms**, two orders of magnitude below the
tightest budget considered. Nothing below is a platform artefact.

### Result

| Model | p50 | p95 | p99 | p99.9 | inference p50 | Min conforming budget |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| tree | 1.81 | 3.51 | 4.76 | 6.09 | 0.18 | **5 ms** |
| logreg | 2.45 | 4.40 | 6.51 | 13.43 | 0.63 | **10 ms** |
| xgboost | 3.83 | 6.19 | 7.29 | 11.39 | 0.96 | **10 ms** |
| mlp | 2.25 | 4.95 | 8.17 | 13.98 | 0.58 | **10 ms** |
| hgb | 53.16 | 74.76 | 94.62 | 446.06 | 41.96 | **100 ms** |
| rf | 93.41 | 124.55 | 378.05 | 1034.91 | 90.79 | **500 ms** |

### The budget sweep is the contribution

| Budget | Conforming architectures |
|---|---|
| 1 ms | none |
| 5 ms | tree |
| **10 ms** | tree, logreg, xgboost, mlp |
| 100 ms | + hgb |
| 1000 ms | all |

O-RAN specifies the near-real-time control loop over **10 ms to 1 s**. The same six
measurements yield four different papers depending only on which line the author
draws. Prior work does exactly this: one study assumes 10 ms and reports
conformance, another assumes 1000 ms and reports conformance with a p99 of ~140 ms —
a measurement that fails the first budget by 14×.

Reporting the crossing point per architecture converts an authoring choice into a
measurement, and it is the quantity an operator actually needs.

### This contradicts the draft's claim C9

The draft says *"only the gradient-boosted tree ensemble meets the 10 ms 99th-percentile
budget"*. Measured: at 10 ms, **four of six** architectures conform, and the fastest is
a plain decision tree, not XGBoost. **C9 must be rewritten to the measured result.**

### What C10 is and is not evidenced by

The `feature_marshal` stage measured here is DataFrame-to-array overhead at 0.2% of
p50. That is **not** evidence for C10 ("feature construction dominates latency") and
must not be quoted as such. The number C10 needs is packet-level feature extraction
cost, which is measured separately against the exporter.

## 4. Bug found and fixed before it reached a result

The first dedup policy dropped repeated Zeek `uid`s, on the reasonable-sounding
theory that a `uid` is unique per connection so a repeat meant the summary had
concatenated captures without namespacing.

Checking rather than assuming showed it was wrong: same-`uid` rows differ in
`http_trans_depth`, `files_total_bytes` and `is_GET_mthd`. They are distinct HTTP
transactions inside one TCP connection, produced by the `conn.log`/`http.log`/
`files.log` merge. The policy discarded **35,916 real observations, 98% of them
benign**, and pushed apparent attack prevalence from 94.6% to 96.7%.

Corrected to exact-duplicate removal only (83,635 rows, 4.85%). Same-`uid` rows share
an `src_ip`, so they cannot straddle an `src_ip`-grouped split.

**Why this matters beyond the fix:** the wrong policy would have inflated the
measured class imbalance, which in turn inflates every alert-burden number in §2. It
was caught by inspecting the data rather than by any test, which is an argument for
the artefact-level verification gate that EXP-001 imposed.
